Section 02

Why this keeps happening

Root cause

  • Every discipline — SEO, Meta Ads, Google Ads, content, schema, backlinks, GBP — is handled by one person, manually, with no team to absorb volume
  • Growth tasks need uninterrupted blocks of time to finish. Maintenance fires interrupt before that block ever happens
  • There is no delegation layer — every task, large or small, strategic or administrative, routes through the same person
  • Result: growth work restarts instead of finishing, day after day

Section 02B · Maintenance

The "maintenance" work isn't optional

It's not neglect of growth work — it's what has to happen first, or growth work has nothing solid to stand on.

Forced, not chosen

  • Payment failures instantly halt Google Ads spend — every hour down is lost lead volume
  • Blocked NAP/citation access stalls local visibility silently, with no error to notice
  • Quality Score drops raise cost-per-lead immediately if left unaddressed
  • Thank-you page miscounting organic leads as paid corrupts every ROI number until fixed
  • Tracking/CSS bugs mean decisions get made on wrong numbers until caught
Skip these and growth work doesn't just slow down — it becomes unmeasurable, or the ad budget stops converting at all. This is why maintenance can't be deprioritized, even though it competes for the same hours as growth work.

Section 04

Tools and interns solve two different problems

Tools — solve a speed problem

  • Research and analysis that takes days gets compressed into hours
  • Data is pulled directly instead of gathered manually, page by page
  • Semrush (research/tracking) + Claude (drafting, synthesis, SOPs)

Interns — solve a capacity problem

  • Even with faster tools, someone still has to execute
  • Sending outreach, fixing listings, pasting schema, correcting data
  • This is what removes execution volume from one person's day
Neither alone is enough: faster research doesn't help if the same person still executes everything after. More hands alone doesn't help if research still takes days per competitor. Both are needed together.

Section 10 · Webinar Blocker

The one number we're missing

Influencer collab — the gap

  • Umair proposed ₹5,000/admission for an influencer collab; stakeholders will only approve up to ₹1,000/admission
  • Umair's assessment: ₹1,000 is very unlikely to attract a serious education influencer, especially against competitors offering more
At ₹1,000/lead, if it takes 2 leads to convert 1 admission, that's a ₹2,000 CAC per admission. Whether that's profitable depends entirely on actual revenue/margin per NEET admission — a number not yet in hand.
The ask: "What's our actual revenue per NEET admission, so we can calculate the real breakeven CAC — instead of debating a lead-cost number in isolation?" Everything else in this section — video budget, influencer terms — is downstream of this number.
Section 10 · Webinar Blocker

Why we can't know the winning creative in advance

The plan

  • Record up to 10 experimental videos — not static ads — across a range of hooks and angles
  • We don't know in advance which creative will perform; this requires real experimentation, not a guess
  • Static ads alone won't produce the signal needed — this is specifically why video is being proposed over static creative
A bounded commitment, not open-ended spend: 10 concepts, tested over a fixed window, capped at a fixed total budget — no single video judged a failure until at least a minimum number have run. This is what makes an experimentation budget approvable.
Section 10 · Webinar Blocker

What the webinar actually is

The strongest, most ready-to-use asset in this section — a one-minute explanation of the real conversion mechanic.

In one minute

"We're not selling the webinar. We're using it to build trust with NEET students who are already warm — they've shown interest through our ads. In the session, we help them directly: real strategy, real guidance, things they can use whether or not they join us. Kids don't make the enrollment decision or hold the budget — parents do. So our job in the room isn't to pitch, it's to make the student walk out impressed enough to go home and tell their parents 'this is where I want to study.' That's the actual conversion event — not a sign-up in the room."

Section 10 · Webinar Blocker

How to convince someone to actually register

  • Make the value concrete, not generic — "learn the exact 3-bucket error method toppers use," not "free NEET webinar"
  • Use the burning-questions hook framework already built for the July webinar — pick 3 of the 12 sharpest questions per ad as hooks
  • Borrow authority — lead with a named faculty member and their real results, not just "Elite IIT"
  • Lower registration friction — one field (WhatsApp number) if possible, no long form
  • Real urgency, not fake scarcity — tie it to the actual exam-cycle timing, not manufactured "only 10 seats left"
  • Speak past the student to the parent in at least one line of copy — the parent is the actual decision-maker
  • Build a reminder sequence (confirm → day-before → hour-before) — registration isn't attendance
